# Next release (post-1.0)

- Dynamic (JKL) trimming now previews the trimmed result live on the Program monitor: the monitor shows the trimmed clip's first frame (left edge) or last frame (right edge) as the edge moves. Nothing is written until K commits it as one undo step; Esc restores the project and playhead exactly. At a cut (roll or ripple with a neighbouring clip) the monitor shows Premiere-style two-up: the outgoing clip's last frame beside the incoming clip's first frame.
- Sequence pixel aspect ratio is now applied to titles, text, shapes, SVG, stickers, captions and particles in export, the Program monitor (frame shown at the display aspect, overlays and video laid out in display space) and graphic template thumbnails, so a circle stays a circle in DV NTSC (0.9091), HDV (1.333) and anamorphic (2.0) sequences. Verified on real pixels in `apps/web/e2e/sequence-par-overlays.spec.ts`.
- Sequence pixel aspect ratio, second pass: the WebGPU preview compositor now lays each layer out in display space (rotation, scale, position, anchor, then the 1/PAR squeeze), matching Canvas2D and export, so a rotated layer is no longer a parallelogram in DV NTSC, HDV or anamorphic sequences. The GPU layer matrix also now takes rotation in degrees (clockwise) and position in canvas pixels with y down, like the other renderers. Blur, glow, shadow and other effects on overlays (export) and on adjustment layers (monitor and export) run on a display-space copy of the frame, so their spread is isotropic where it is looked at. The Program monitor's CSS blur on a video clip is filtered in a display-space layer too, so a blurred clip is isotropic in the monitor as in export. Verified on real pixels in the WebGPU and software-fallback Playwright projects (`apps/web/e2e/sequence-par-geometry.spec.ts`, `fallback-sequence-par-geometry.spec.ts`).
- Project packages now embed WOFF2 fonts (as-is, when the OS/2 licence allows it) and report restricted ones, in every browser and the desktop app. Licences are read with native `DecompressionStream("brotli")` where available, the desktop shell's Node zlib bridge, or a bundled Brotli decoder (`brotli-dec-wasm`, MIT/Apache-2.0, loaded on first use as a separate ~210 KB chunk).
- The Effects and Transitions panels now preview every card on your own footage instead of decorative artwork. Each card shows a still poster of the frame under the playhead (the selected clip when the playhead is inside it, else the topmost video clip there, else the nearest video clip; a bundled neutral sample scene when the project has no video). Effect cards are rendered by the editor's own effect engines (the same canvas passes as the Canvas2D fallback renderer and export, default parameters, LUT included); transition cards blend two moments of the same footage with the real transition engine (the old amber shapes, including the Iris circle, are gone). Hovering or keyboard-focusing a card plays a looped ~2 s window (12 fps) with the effect or transition applied; leaving stops it, and `prefers-reduced-motion` keeps the poster. Chroma key shows a real key of the footage's own dominant colour (or green when the footage has a green screen) over a transparency checkerboard and Stabilize shows its stabilization crop; both carry a small badge ("Sample key", "Needs analysis") so the card is honest about what it demonstrates. Film grain is shown at 2.5x its amount because per-pixel noise averages away at card size. The footage window is decoded once at card size and shared by all cards; posters render lazily for visible cards only, one at a time in genuine idle periods, and no preview work starts while the playhead is being scrubbed or played (it resumes 300 ms after it settles). Posters live in their own 32 MB LRU and hover frames in a separate 16 MB one, rendered a few frames ahead of playback, so sweeping the pointer across cards never evicts other cards' posters. Verified on real pixels in `apps/web/e2e/effect-previews.spec.ts` (gpu) and `fallback-effect-previews.spec.ts` (no WebGPU, software).
- Transition card posters now rest on a frame that shows what the transition does: Dip to black / white, Flash Cut, Film Burn, Light Leak, Flip and the eased dissolves no longer open on a solid square or a single shot (hover still sweeps 0 to 1). The Effects chips have honest states: "No effects match" only appears for a non-empty search, Chroma key is a card in the Keying grid (with its Configure button below), and the search placeholder reflects the active chip (Audio disables it). The preview pipeline and the About, AI connection, Compress, Collaboration, shortcuts and sticker dialogs now load on first use, taking the editor chunk from 504.8 to about 484 KB gzip. Fixed a crash when switching between the Effects and Transitions panels (preview renderer disposal).
- Transition cards now blend your footage towards the next clip on the timeline (the first frame of the clip that follows the one under the playhead), or towards the bundled sample scene when the project has a single clip, so wipes, slides, pushes, splits, irises and spins each show their own geometry on a talking-head clip instead of two near-identical moments. Radial Wipe and Spin rest on a frame that tells them apart from Wipe Right and from each other, and a failed preview chunk fetch now degrades to plain cards instead of taking the panel down.
- Looks and LUTs in the Color tab now show their real result on your footage. The 30 built-in looks, imported LUTs, saved looks and the white balance presets are cards with a poster of the playhead frame graded by the same effect and colour pipeline "apply" uses (hover or keyboard focus plays it; reduced motion keeps the poster). The two LUT sections are merged into one "Looks and LUTs" section with labelled category tabs, plain-language adjustment names instead of raw effect ids, an empty state and icon buttons; a look shows the selected ring only when it is really applied. Code is lazy-loaded (editor chunk 505.0 to 500.4 KB gzip). Verified on real pixels in `apps/web/e2e/looks-luts.spec.ts` and `fallback-looks-luts.spec.ts`.
- Motion presets, text animation, emphasis, particle presets and the shader browser now preview on your own footage (the selected clip, or the sample scene when the project has none), drawn by the same engines the Program monitor and export use: the preset's real keyframes, your own selected text through the title engine, the particle sampler, and the real shader output. Cards play on hover or keyboard focus, keep a still poster for reduced motion, and a failed preview says "Preview unavailable" instead of a stand-in gradient.
- Particle and emphasis cards are representative at rest: a particle poster is the instant where the preset is most visibly at work (ambient dust and snow start in their steady state, dots are enlarged for card size and the footage is dimmed so grey dust reads), and an emphasis poster is a 2 x 2 filmstrip of real engine frames (rest, peak, trough), so Pulse, Heartbeat, Glow, Breathe and Flash are told apart without hovering. Verified on real pixels against the footage in `apps/web/e2e/inspector-previews.spec.ts`.
- Graphics > Overlays cards and every template surface now show the real thing instead of decorative art. Each overlay card (Progress bar, Letterbox 2.39, Follow callout, Frame border) is the overlay resolved from its editing template and drawn by the title / graphics engines over the footage frame at the playhead (the bundled sample scene when the project has no visual media), with a poster at rest, a short hover / keyboard-focus animation and reduced motion keeping the poster; the renderer is a lazily loaded chunk on the shared effect-preview pipeline. Making the cards honest exposed three bugs in what "apply" produced, now fixed: template shapes ignored their width / height (a letterbox bar was a small square), contrast grades were written as +0.08 instead of the 1.08 multiplier (they greyed the clip), and the Follow callout showed a literal {{label}}; the Progress bar now grows from its left edge. Every built-in template has a rendered thumbnail (the template applied through the real engines over the sample scene, committed under `public/template-thumbs`, regenerate with `apps/web/scripts/render-template-thumbs.mjs`; saved and cloud templates are rendered lazily and cached), shared by the welcome gallery, Templates tabs and preview modal through one `TemplateThumb`. Welcome cards no longer collapse the thumbnail to a chip, rows keep even heights, and platform tabs with no templates are hidden. Verified on real pixels in `apps/web/e2e/overlays-templates.spec.ts` and `fallback-overlays-templates.spec.ts`.
- Overlay and template polish, second pass: the Frame Opacity and Watermark Opacity template controls now work (template blend opacity is converted to the project's 0-100 scale and honoured by the 2D shape and text renderers in preview, export and the cards; shape transform and animation opacity is now also applied by the graphics engine on export). Overlay cards in vertical or square projects show the whole frame fitted inside the 16:9 card instead of a centre crop, lay out at card size rather than project resolution (no 4K canvases per hover frame) and share their scratch engines through a lock so concurrent cards cannot clear each other. Template text now wraps inside the frame (the Quote Card quote is no longer clipped), a failed sample-scene fetch no longer disables runtime thumbnails for the session, and a unit test fails when a built-in template changes without its bundled thumbnail being regenerated.
- Transition cards: Spin and Flip posters rest where the shot is large and legible, and the incoming shot of a transition card now plays (decoded from the next clip) while the card is hovered.

- Looks and LUTs: cards preview on top of the clip's own effects and grade exactly as apply composes them, with a Before / after split and a LUT strength control; category tabs wrap instead of clipping.
- Look, LUT, saved-look and white balance cards: one Before / after switch for the whole Color tab, a Sample badge when a card shows the bundled sample scene, and posters are stamped so tests wait for the real render.
- Particle preset cards: Soft Dissolve now emits across the frame (a visible dissolve, in the preview and on apply), Ambient Dust reads at card size, and every hover loop starts with particles already in flight.
- Overlay and template polish, third pass: Overlays and Graphics > Templates cards in vertical or square projects now place your footage exactly as the Program monitor does (contained and letterboxed on black, never cover-cropped), from the real decoded playhead frame at its own aspect instead of the small import thumbnail, so they are crisp. Overlay cards are full panel width so a 12 px progress bar is a visible few pixels. The template modal hero plays the template's own animation on hover or keyboard focus (reduced motion keeps the still). Preview SVG and sticker clips now honour blend opacity like export, stickers can take a blend mode and opacity from the inspector, and motion instances mirror their 0..1 opacity on the clip's 0-100 Blend Opacity scale. The save-template dialog plays its exit animation. Verified in real Chrome: saved and cloud template thumbnails, a 4K long-task trace (no long tasks), and an export-versus-preview pixel test for blend opacity (`blend-opacity.spec.ts`).
- Clip markers and master-clip markers: M in the Source monitor (or the new Add marker button) drops a master-clip marker on the media; Alt+Shift+M on the timeline drops a clip marker on the selected clip (or the clip under the playhead). Marker times are stored in source seconds, so they follow the footage through trims, speed changes and reverse: master markers appear on every timeline instance at the right offset (flags along the clip's lower edge, range markers as bars; ticks on the Source monitor scrubber). The Markers panel has a Sequence / Clips switch; Clips lists each instance's markers at timeline time (click to seek, double-click to rename, delete), with the rows of selected clips highlighted. All edits are one undoable `clipmarker/set` action. XMEML export writes clip and master markers into each clipitem (source frames) and import reads them back as clip markers. Unit tests cover offset mapping with in point, speed, reverse and range markers (`editing/clip-markers.test.ts`); e2e in `apps/web/e2e/clip-markers.spec.ts`. Not carried: FCPXML / OTIO / AAF clip markers, markers on nested-sequence clips, and variable-speed (speed ramp) mapping, which uses the constant-speed map.

- Source monitor history and Reverse Match Frame: the Source monitor title is now a recent-clips dropdown (last 10 clips loaded this session, most recent first) and Shift+F (Edit menu, or "Find in sequence" in the Source monitor) finds the frame showing in the Source monitor in the active sequence and moves the playhead there, selecting the clip. The topmost video track wins (hidden tracks skipped, audio-only sources fall back to audio tracks), the instance nearest the playhead wins within a track, and speed changes, reverse and speed ramps are inverted with the same mapping the renderer uses. A frame that is not in the sequence leaves the playhead alone and shows a toast. Match Frame (F) is unchanged.

- Planar tracking (Effects tab > Planar Tracking): track a flat surface by its four corners and pin a clip above it. Drag the corner quad on the Program monitor (or type the corners), then track the whole clip, forward or backward from the playhead, with progress and cancel. Shi-Tomasi features inside the quad are followed with pyramidal Lucas-Kanade in a worker; every feature remembers its anchor-frame position, so a seeded RANSAC homography fitted anchor to current rejects occluders without accumulating drift. Frames where the surface is lost are flagged in the quality strip, and the corners of any frame can be edited by hand and re-tracked from there. "Pin Clip to Surface" adds a Corner Pin effect to the chosen clip with per-frame corner keyframes (`fx:` instance keyframes), so preview and export render the same pixels deterministically, and everything is one undo step. Homography estimation, RANSAC and the tracker have unit tests (`planar-homography.test.ts`, `planar-track.test.ts`); the browser flow is `apps/web/e2e/planar-track.spec.ts`.

- White Balance eyedropper (Color tab > White Balance): pick a neutral grey or white area on the Program monitor and Temperature / Tint are set so that sample becomes neutral, like Lumetri's WB Selector. Choose a single pixel or a 5x5 average; the cursor becomes a crosshair with a footprint box and a live "Temp / Tint" read-out, Esc or right-click cancels, and a click applies and disarms. The sample is the clip as the white balance stage sees it: on a plain clip that is the Program canvas, and when later grade stages (tone, curves, wheels, LUT, look on the default Premiere-order pipeline) or an active white balance would change the picture, the monitor re-renders that stage input once, preview-only, while the visible frame is held still, so contrast or a look never skews the result. It works the same on the GPU and Canvas2D paths, picking again never compounds, and the pick is exactly one undo step. A strongly coloured sample is applied but flagged "not neutral" in the read-out and the toast. Clipped black / white samples and keyframed white balance are refused with a message. The neutralising maths is a closed form over the same offsets the preview, export and HDR grade use (`white-balance-eyedropper.test.ts` checks it against `prepareHdrGrade`). Verified on real footage with a cast and a contrast stage (v1 and v2 pipelines) in `apps/web/e2e/color-wb-eyedropper.spec.ts` and `fallback-color-wb-eyedropper.spec.ts`.
- Camera log input presets. Interpret Footage has a new "Camera input (log)" group and the Color tab has a "Camera input" picker (above the Input LUT) with built-in, formula-based transforms from the published manufacturer specifications, no LUT files: Sony S-Log3 / S-Gamut3.Cine, Panasonic V-Log / V-Gamut, ARRI LogC3 (EI 800) / AWG3 and LogC4 / AWG4, Canon C-Log3 / Cinema Gamut, Nikon N-Log (Rec.2020), Fujifilm F-Log and F-Log2 (Rec.2020) and Blackmagic Film Gen5 / Wide Gamut. Each converts log to scene-linear (18% grey = 0.18), maps the camera gamut to Rec.709 (matrices derived from the published primaries) and rolls highlights off with the shared shoulder, so footage looks neutral and exposure-correct rather than graded. The same curve runs in the preview, scopes and export CPU input transform, the CPU HDR reference and the WebGL2 HDR compositor shader (generated from the same constants), so they cannot disagree. The setting is the media item's Interpret Footage colour setting: one undoable step shared by every clip of that footage. Verified by unit tests against published reference values (`packages/core/src/color/camera-log.test.ts`: 0%, 18% grey and 90% white code values, segment joins, gamut matrices, shader source evaluated against the TypeScript curve) and on real pixels in `apps/web/e2e/camera-log.spec.ts` (WebGL2 shader against the CPU reference for all nine curves; the picker against the CPU transform).
- Voice-over recording is now a proper Premiere-style tool (Audio > Voiceover). Pick the input from the device list (remembered between sessions, with a fallback to the system default when a saved microphone is unplugged), watch the live input meter (and "Check level" before you commit), choose the destination audio track, set a 0 to 5 s pre-roll and press Record: the timeline plays from the pre-roll start while the countdown runs, recording begins exactly at the playhead and playback is muted for the take (optional, on by default) so speakers cannot bleed into the microphone. With In and Out marks set, "Punch in / out" records only between them and stops by itself. Each take is named "Voice Over Take N", listed in the Takes list, and lands as an ordinary audio clip; recording over a range that is already taken stacks the new take on a new "Voice Over N" track instead of overlapping, and one undo removes the whole take (track, name and clip together). Desktop: the app now declares the microphone and camera to macOS (usage descriptions plus the audio-input and camera hardened-runtime entitlements), asks the operating system for access before the first take, explains a denied or restricted state with an "Open microphone settings" shortcut, and only grants media permissions to its own pages. Unit tests cover the timing and placement rules (`voiceover-logic.test.ts`) and the desktop permission policy; `apps/web/e2e/voiceover.spec.ts` records against Chrome's fake microphone.
- Audio output device picker and solo-safe tracks. Choose the speakers or headphones used for preview in Settings > General > Audio Output or in the Audio Mixer header (also `AudioOutputSelect` in the mixer). The choice is applied with `AudioContext.setSinkId` (Chromium, Electron), or through a hidden media element's `setSinkId` where only that exists, is remembered between sessions, and when the device is unplugged playback continues on the system default with a toast; it switches back (with a second toast) when the device returns. Exports render offline and are unaffected. Solo Safe (the SF button on each mixer strip, "Solo Safe" in the track header menus, `set_track_solo_safe` for the agent) keeps a track playing while other tracks are soloed, in live playback, scrubbing and the export mixdown; mute still wins. It is one undo step and saved with the project (`track.soloSafe`, absent on older projects = off). The mute/solo/solo-safe rule lives in `packages/core/src/audio/track-audibility.ts` and is covered by an exhaustive matrix test; verified end to end in `apps/web/e2e/audio-output-solo-safe.spec.ts`.

- Echo and Posterize Time, Premiere's two missing Time effects, are in the Effects panel under a new Time filter (and the inspector effect picker). Posterize Time holds the clip at a fixed frame rate (0 is off; the default is Premiere's 1 fps): the frame shown is the one at the start of the held interval on the clip-local timeline (`floor(t * fps) / fps`, float-safe, through the clip's speed), so it is the very frame the unprocessed clip shows there. Echo (echo time, number of echoes up to 32, starting intensity, decay, operator Add / Maximum / Minimum / Screen / Composite in Back / Composite in Front) combines the current frame with the frames at `t - k * echoTime` (a positive echo time reaches back; the default is 0.033 s, one frame at 30 fps), weighted `startIntensity * decay^k`; echoes that would fall outside the clip show nothing. Effects above the Echo in the stack are applied to every echo frame (sampled at that frame's own time), the ones below see the combined frame. Both are keyframable, and the frame choice is a pure function of the clip-local time, so preview, scrubbing, the render cache and export pick identical frames. Clips with these effects play through the same engine renderer export uses. The Effects cards play real footage: Echo rests a few frames into the window with its trail (and the trail builds up from the first frame on hover), Posterize Time holds frames while it plays. Verified on real pixels in `apps/web/e2e/time-effects.spec.ts` (gpu) and `fallback-time-effects.spec.ts`: Posterize Time equals the plain frame at the start of the interval, an exported file holds the posterized frames, and Echo matches the reference composite of the plain frames for all six operators, in preview mode and export mode, in any render order.

- Timecode and Clip Name burn-in. Two new effects under Generate in the Effects panel and the inspector's Add Effect picker: Timecode draws sequence, source-file or clip timecode (drop-frame aware for 29.97 and 59.94, a Time display override, frames and feet + frames formats, an offset in frames, an optional second-field mark and a label) and Clip Name draws the clip name, file name (extension optional) or sequence name with an optional prefix. Both have position (nine anchors plus margin and offset), size, opacity, text colour and a background box. Text is resolved per frame from the clip's place on the timeline and in its source (speed and freeze frames respected) and drawn with Canvas2D at a fixed digit advance, so the monitor, playback, the Canvas2D fallback and export show identical digits for the same frame; adjustment layers and text, shape, SVG and sticker clips carry them too. Export > Burn in timecode is a one-click switch in the Export dialog (every video tab) that draws the sequence timecode, or the topmost clip's source timecode, over the whole picture including captions, with a position choice; it re-renders every frame (no stream copy). Verified on real pixels in the monitor, in `VideoEngine.renderFrame` and in an exported MP4 decoded with ffmpeg (`apps/web/e2e/burn-in.spec.ts` gpu, `fallback-burn-in.spec.ts` no-WebGPU and software), with unit tests for 29.97 and 59.94 drop-frame formatting and boundaries.

- Replace edit and Fit to Fill in the Source monitor (Premiere's remaining Source-monitor edit commands). Replace (Shift+G, button, Edit menu) swaps the footage of the selected timeline clip, else the topmost clip under the playhead (targeted tracks first), for the Source monitor's clip. The clip keeps its id, place, length, transitions and links; the Source playhead frame is lined up with the timeline playhead, or the source In (media start when unmarked) lands on the clip's first frame when the playhead is outside the clip. A Keep FX toggle keeps (default) or resets the clip's effects, keyframes, transform, audio settings and fades; the old footage's speed, ramps, freeze frames and stabilization never carry over, and linked audio of the same footage is replaced with the picture. It refuses, changing nothing, when the source is too short, the aligned frame is before the media start, a track is locked, the kinds do not match or a restricted subclip would be exceeded. Fit to Fill (Alt+Shift+F, button, Edit menu) retimes the source In/Out range (the whole media when unmarked) to exactly fill the timeline In/Out range and overwrites it onto the patched / targeted tracks, with video and audio patched to separate linked tracks at the same speed; audio keeps its pitch through the existing pitch-correction default. The needed speed is shown in the confirmation and refused outside the 10%-2000% clip speed limits (now one shared constant). Both are single undo steps through the timeline action path; neither ripples, so Sync Lock has nothing to shift (locked tracks are refused). Fixed a Replace-blocking validation bug: a footage swap was bounded by the old media's duration. Covered by `replace-fit.test.ts` and `apps/web/e2e/replace-fit.spec.ts`.
- Clip > Audio Channels… (Premiere "Modify > Audio Channels"): map a clip's source channels onto mono, stereo or 5.1 clip channels for one clip or a whole selection. The dialog has a Preset (Use file, Mono = first channel, Stereo), and 5.1 sources keep all six channels for the mapping in preview, waveforms and export (stereo sequences included), a clip channel format, a source-channel grid with Mix and Off columns, quick Left only / Right only / Swap L/R buttons, and Split to mono clips, which breaks any clip with multichannel audio (audio files and video with embedded audio) out to in-sync mono clips on separate tracks. Everything is one undo step (`audio/setChannelMap`, multi-clip safe). The mapping is applied to the decoded source, so preview playback (realtime graph, scrub grains, stretched renders), the offline mixdown used by export, compound audio, metering and the timeline waveform (recomputed from the mapped channels) all agree. Preferences > Audio > Default audio channels stamps new imports with Mono or Stereo (Use file by default). Matrix, action and undo covered by `channel-map.test.ts` and `channel-actions.test.ts`; real mixdown checked in `apps/web/e2e/audio-channels.spec.ts`.

- Speaker diarization: Transcript > "Detect speakers" labels the caption cues "Speaker 1..N" fully on device (no upload). Each cue's audio is voice-printed in a worker (MFCC statistics plus pitch, no model download), clustered by average-linkage cosine distance (Auto finds the speaker count from the clearest gap in the merge distances, validated on real two-voice AAC audio; or fix the count), and short A-B-A flickers are smoothed; cues with no usable audio take the nearest speaker in time. Speakers are saved with the project (`Project.speakers`), shown with a colour in the transcript, renameable (renaming onto another speaker merges them), reassignable per passage, and each speaker's colour and caption position (Top / Centre / Bottom) are baked onto their cues so preview and every export render the same. Detect, rename, reassign and recolour are each one undo step (`speaker/apply`). Re-running Detect keeps renamed speakers (a voice maps back onto the speaker it overlaps most), drops stale profiles, and clearing a passage's speaker restores its original caption look. The speaker list is one collapsed line (Edit expands it). The panel and docs/known-limitations.md state that matching is approximate (similar voices can merge or split, overlapping speech is not separated); a clip with no audio track gets its own message. Unit tests use synthetic embeddings and synthetic voices; e2e (real two-voice fixture, Auto detection, rename, clear, re-detect) in `apps/web/e2e/diarization.spec.ts`.

- Caption translation: Transcript panel > Translate turns the active caption track into a new track in another language (timing, speaker and style of every cue are kept; word timings are dropped because translated words no longer line up). It uses the AI model connected for the AI editor when there is one, otherwise the browser's on-device Translator API (Chrome 138+, language packs download on first use), and says plainly when neither is available instead of producing stand-in text. Translation runs in batches with a progress bar and Cancel, commits nothing unless every batch succeeds, and the new track is one undo step.

- Ultra Key (Keying category): a Premiere-style chroma keyer as a keyframable effect. Matte generation (transparency, highlight, shadow, tolerance, pedestal), matte cleanup (choke, soften, contrast, mid point), spill suppression (desaturate, range, spill, luma), colour correction (saturation, hue, luminance) and Composite / Alpha channel / Colour channel / Source outputs. The inspector has a key-colour eyedropper that samples the Program monitor, and the Effects card previews the real key on your footage. One shared implementation drives the WebGL2 shader, the Canvas2D fallback and export; unit tests cover the key math and Playwright (gpu and no-WebGPU projects) keys a generated green-screen clip and compares the shader to the CPU reference (`ultra-key.test.ts`, `ultra-key.spec.ts`, `fallback-ultra-key.spec.ts`).
- Unified background-task panel: a status bar at the bottom of the editor shows a live count and progress; clicking it opens one Background tasks popover listing every long-running job with progress, ETA and cancel / pause / resume / retry where the owning system supports it: export-queue jobs (queued jobs pause and resume with the queue) and direct exports, proxy generation (in-app and desktop native), desktop copy-to-project, local caption and multicam transcription, media imports (Cancel stops before the media is added; Retry re-runs the same row and adds the media), scene edit detection, beat detection and motion tracking. Finished runs stay as history, failures show their error. It is a presentation layer over one `BackgroundTaskRegistry` in core (`tasks/task-registry`): existing job systems mirror their state in and keep owning the work, so there is no second job system.

- Keyframe temporal interpolation (Premiere): right-click a keyframe (graph or list; the menu ticks the current type) or use the Interpolation menu to apply Linear, Bezier, Auto Bezier, Continuous Bezier, Hold, Ease In or Ease Out to the selection as one undo step, to both sides of each key or to its In or Out side only (the Interpolation side menu). Every key has an independent incoming and outgoing type, shown in the list as "In X · Out Y". Auto Bezier derives smooth, overshoot-free tangents and re-derives them when a neighbour moves; Continuous Bezier keeps both handles of a key tangent-linked while you drag either one. A smooth side only ever rewrites the handle it owns, so a hand-tuned handle at the other end of a shared segment is never overwritten, and dragging a handle on an Auto Bezier key turns it Continuous Bezier. Types bake into the existing bezier handles, so preview, export and the graph evaluate identically; older projects open unchanged (new optional `interpolationIn` / `interpolationOut` fields, gated by portable-edit version). Unit tests in `keyframe-interpolation.test.ts`, e2e in `keyframe-graph-editor.spec.ts`.
- Caption import now also reads TTML / DFXP / IMSC1 (clock, offset, frame and tick times, parent-relative timing, referential styles and regions mapped to the caption style and cue position, `ttm:agent` speakers) and YouTube SBV; SBV joins SRT, WebVTT, TTML, SCC and EBU-STL as an export format. Text files are decoded as UTF-8, UTF-16 (BOM) or Windows-1252, and every skipped cue or block is listed in the Transcript panel's notes with its line number.
- Multi-clip Speed/Duration with ripple. Clip > Speed/Duration... (Cmd/Ctrl+Alt+R) sets speed, duration, direction and Maintain Audio Pitch for every selected clip (linked audio follows) as one undo step, with Premiere's "Ripple edit, shifting trailing clips": trailing clips on each edited track shift by what the clip gained or lost, unlocked sync-locked tracks and markers open or close the same span, and shrinking is blocked when a sync-locked track holds media in the span it would close. With several clips, Duration is the combined length and scales each clip by the same factor. The Rate Stretch tool now works on a multi-selection: dragging one selected clip's edge scales every selected clip and ripples the rest (one undo step, bounded to 0.1x-20x), with a ghost on every stretched clip; hold Alt while dragging to stretch without rippling (a clip then cannot grow into its neighbour). Linked audio always follows a speed change, even with Linked Selection off. Core planner `planClipSpeedEdit` in `packages/core/src/editing/clip-speed-edit.ts` (unit tests alongside, `apps/web/e2e/ripple-speed.spec.ts` for the UI).
- Encoder picker and proxy presets: the export dialog has an Encoder choice (Auto, Hardware, Software) for H.264, H.265 and AV1. On desktop it lists what the ffmpeg sidecar verified on this machine (VideoToolbox, NVENC, Quick Sync, AMF) and in the browser what WebCodecs reports; a choice the machine cannot run is disabled with the reason, and Hardware never silently falls back. Proxy presets (ProRes Proxy, H.264 1/2, H.264 1/4, DNxHR LB) are selectable in the Proxies panel, apply to new imports, are saved with the project, and are also offered in a Create Proxies dialog; ProRes and DNxHR proxies get an H.264 preview twin so the toggle-proxies button keeps working.
- Keyframe graph (Premiere): spatial interpolation for position (Linear, Bezier, Auto Bezier, Continuous Bezier) with the motion path and its handles drawn on the Program monitor; a speed graph with draggable influence and speed handles beside the value graph; box select and moving several keyframes at once; dragging a handle on an Auto Bezier key now makes it Continuous Bezier, as in Premiere; keyframes paste across clips (effect keys follow onto the target's matching effect). Older `ease-in` / `ease-out` keys open as exact Bezier handles with identical values. New agent tools: `list_clip_keyframes`, `set_keyframe_interpolation`, `set_keyframe_handles`, `sample_keyframes`, `copy_keyframes_to_clip`. Tests: `spatial-path.test.ts`, `keyframe-analytic.test.ts`, `keyframe-migration.test.ts`, `registry.keyframe-graph.test.ts`, `e2e/keyframe-spatial-path.spec.ts`.
- Immersive video (VR / 360): equirectangular footage is detected on import (Spherical Video V1 / V2 metadata), sequence and clip VR Properties, a Program monitor VR view (drag, scroll, reset; view only), VR Rotate Sphere, VR Projection (Reframe to flat, little planet, fisheye), VR Blur and VR Plane to Sphere as keyframable effects (GPU and CPU with identical results, angle keyframes take the shorter arc), spherical metadata (`st3d` / `sv3d`) written into MP4 / MOV exports of a VR sequence, and agent tools `set_sequence_vr_properties`, `set_clip_vr_properties`, `get_vr_properties` and `reframe_360_to_flat`. See docs/known-limitations.md, "Immersive video".

- Immersive video, second batch: VR Sharpen, VR Glow, VR Chromatic Aberrations, VR Digital Glitch and VR De-Noise (Effects > Immersive; seam-, pole- and stereo-aware, keyframable, GPU and CPU with the same maths, preview identical to export), a VR video view in the Source monitor, the Spherical Video V1 XML written next to V2 on VR exports, and an initial view direction (heading / pitch / roll) in Sequence VR Properties. Ambisonic (SA3D) metadata is not written; see known limitations.
